Donghyeok Park

Reliable Autonomy, Manipulation, and Physical AI for Real-World Robotics

I am a robotics engineer at Korea Aerospace Industries (KAI), where I develop and deploy robotic systems for real-world aerospace manufacturing. My work spans humanoid logistics, collaborative-robot automation, vision-guided manufacturing, and contact-rich surface finishing.

My research interests lie in reliable autonomous robotic systems that integrate perception, learning, planning, and control to operate under uncertainty. I am particularly interested in robot perception and scene understanding, manipulation and physical interaction, localization and navigation, multi-robot systems, and intelligent manufacturing.

Drawing on experience with robots operating outside controlled laboratory settings, I aim to study how model-based reasoning and data-driven learning can be combined to improve robustness, adaptability, and safety. My long-term goal is to develop robotic systems that can be validated and trusted in complex environments such as manufacturing, logistics, and aerospace operations.

Research Interests

My research focuses on reliable autonomy for robots that must perceive, reason, and physically interact with complex and uncertain environments. Rather than treating perception, planning, and control as isolated modules, I am interested in how these capabilities can be integrated and validated as complete robotic systems.

  • Robot perception and state estimation: vision-based perception, 3D scene understanding, object pose estimation, localization, and environment-aware sensing
  • Manipulation and physical interaction: vision-guided, force-aware, contact-rich, cooperative, and bimanual manipulation
  • Planning, control, and learning: motion planning and control under uncertainty, learning-based adaptation, imitation learning, and autonomous decision-making
  • Mobile and multi-robot autonomy: reliable navigation, heterogeneous robot coordination, and task execution in challenging environments
  • Real-world robotic systems: sim-to-real transfer, closed-loop validation, intelligent manufacturing, and scalable deployment in aerospace and industrial environments

I am especially motivated by research that connects algorithmic advances with physical experiments and measurable system performance, enabling robots to move from laboratory demonstrations to dependable real-world operation.

Collaborative R&D Project · May 2026 – Dec 2026

Force-Aware Robotic Polishing of Curved Aerospace Surfaces

Developing a cobot-based surface-finishing process for the aircraft wing. The project uses STEP geometry to analyze curvature-dependent contact conditions and develops repeatable sanding and buffing recipes for complex aerodynamic surfaces.

Key Contributions

  • Analyze wing STEP geometry to characterize local surface curvature and estimate the relationship between applied force and contact pressure
  • Compare expected tool-surface contact behavior with actual contact coverage on curved wing surfaces
  • Develop experimental sanding recipes using Mirka equipment by varying abrasive-paper sequence and the number of passes
  • Develop wool-pad buffing procedures and evaluate the resulting surface finish and gloss through visual inspection
  • Iteratively refine robot process parameters based on physical trials on representative surface geometries

Current Status

The process recipe is under development. Sanding consistency, contact coverage, and final gloss are currently assessed through controlled trials and visual inspection.

Technologies & Methods: Collaborative Robot, STEP/CAD Geometry, Force–Pressure Modeling, Mirka Sanding Equipment, Abrasive Process Planning, Wool-Pad Buffing

Government-Funded R&D Project · Aug 2025–Dec 2025

Humanoid Logistics Automation in Aerospace Production Lines

Developed an autonomy framework for humanoid-driven logistics in an active aerospace manufacturing environment. The system integrated mobile navigation, visual part verification, and perception-driven task execution for handling aerospace components.

Key Contributions

  • Designed and implemented 2D LiDAR SLAM-based navigation for inter-facility logistics
  • Introduced visual landmark correction to reduce accumulated localization drift during repeated operation
  • Integrated YOLOv8-based perception to verify part presence before manipulation tasks
  • Developed perception-driven execution logic for autonomous task decisions
  • Implemented teleoperation-based demonstrations for box-picking and loading sequences

Results

Achieved greater than 96% task success over 31 cumulative hours of operation and improved final-position accuracy from approximately 100–150 mm drift to 5–10 mm through landmark-based correction.

Technologies & Methods: Python, YOLOv8, 2D LiDAR SLAM, ArUco Markers, RGB Vision, Humanoid Robot (RB-Y1), Teleoperation

Government-Funded R&D Project · Jul 2025–Feb 2026

Collaborative-Robot Drilling Automation for Aerospace Assembly

Developed an automated drilling system using AGV-mounted collaborative robots, machine vision, PLC-controlled drilling, and closed-loop pose correction for aerospace assembly operations.

Key Contributions

  • Engineered a multi-robot drilling workflow using three collaborative robots on an AGV platform
  • Implemented vision-based hole detection and process verification
  • Developed a closed-loop pose-correction process before each drilling operation
  • Integrated robot motion with PLC-controlled drilling signals for synchronized execution
  • Built a sequential validation workflow to maintain process consistency across jig-mounted assemblies

Results

Reduced required staffing by approximately 47% and achieved zero out-of-tolerance defects per 1,000 holes during system evaluation, compared with recurring defects in the previous manual process.

Technologies & Methods: Python, Mech-Vision, Machine Vision, Industrial PLC Integration, Collaborative Robots, AGV Systems, Closed-Loop Pose Correction
